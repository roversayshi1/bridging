Title Expansion and Factorisation of Algebraic Expressions
Author AprilDolphin – Lim Wang Sheng
Date 22/12/2025
In this topic, we shall be covering the following concepts
• Expansion of Algebraic Expressions by Simple Multiplication, Algebraic Identity
and “Rainbow” Method of Expansion
• Factoring of Algebraic Expressions involving Simple Factoring, Algebraic
Identities, Grouping and Cross Method
Expansion is the act of multiplying terms in brackets with other terms in order to
remove the brackets completely.
Example 1. [Simple Multiplication]
Expand and simplify the following:
−3(𝑥 − 4)
The above expression can first be expanded as follows by simple multiplication by
ensuring the term outside is multiplied by every term in the bracket. (Be extremely
careful when negative signs are involved.)
−3(𝑥 − 4) = −3(𝑥) − 3(−4) = −3𝑥 + 12
Example 2. [Algebraic Identities]
In this example here, we have to take note of whether the algebraic expression we have
to expand resembles any algebraic identities as follows and if there is resemblance to
apply the identities as follows:
(𝑎 + 𝑏)2 = 𝑎2 + 2𝑎𝑏 + 𝑏2
(𝑎 − 𝑏)2 = 𝑎2 − 2𝑎𝑏 + 𝑏2
(𝑎 + 𝑏)(𝑎 − 𝑏) = 𝑎2 − 𝑏2
Expand and simplify the following:
(a) (3𝑥 + 5)2
(b) (3𝑥 − 5)2
(c) (𝑥 + 5)(𝑥 − 5)
In the case of example 2(a), the question can be solved by applying the algebraic
identity (𝑎 + 𝑏)2 = 𝑎2 + 2𝑎𝑏 + 𝑏2
(3𝑥 + 5)2 = 9𝑥2 + 2(3𝑥)(5) + (5)2 = 9𝑥2 + 30𝑥 + 25
In the case of example 2(b), the question can be solved by applying the algebraic
identity (𝑎 − 𝑏)2 = 𝑎2 − 2𝑎𝑏 + 𝑏2
(3𝑥 − 5)2 = 9𝑥2 − 2(3𝑥)(5) + 52 = 9𝑥2 − 30𝑥 + 25

In the case of example 2(c), the question can be solved by applying the algebraic
identity (𝑎 + 𝑏)(𝑎 − 𝑏) = 𝑎2 − 𝑏2
(𝑥 + 5)(𝑥 − 5) = 𝑥2 − 52 = 𝑥2 − 25
Example 3. [“Rainbow” Method of Expansion]
If the algebraic expression given cannot be expanded by simple multiplication or
algebraic identities applications, we use the 3rd method which is referred to the
“Rainbow” Method of Expansion in this document.
Expand and simplify the Following:
• (2𝑥 + 4)(3𝑥 − 3)
(2𝑥 + 4)(3𝑥 − 3)
As you can see, the lines drawn connect the terms that should be multiplied together
and later to be added or subtracted up accordingly in a row.
(2𝑥 + 4)(3𝑥 − 3) = 6𝑥2 + 12𝑥 − 6𝑥 − 12
= 6𝑥2 + 6𝑥 − 12
Example 4. [Applications of Expansion of Algebraic Expression to Arithmetic Problems]
4(a) Using the algebraic identity (𝑎 + 𝑏)(𝑎 − 𝑏) = 𝑎2 − 𝑏2, evaluate 28 × 32
Since 28 can be rewritten as 30 − 2 and 32 can be rewritten as 30 + 2, it now
resembles the algebraic identity (𝑎 + 𝑏)(𝑎 − 𝑏) and therefore can be evaluated as
follows:
(30 − 2)(30 + 2) = 302 − 22 = 900 − 4 = 896
4(b) Using the algebraic identity (𝑎 + 𝑏)2 = 𝑎2 + 2𝑎𝑏 + 𝑏2, evaluate 152
Since 15 can be rewritten as 10 + 5 and the question demanding us to evaluate 152, it
now resembles the algebraic identity (𝑎 + 𝑏)2 and therefore can be evaluated as
follows:
(10 + 5)2 = 102 + 2(5)(10) + 52 = 100 + 100 + 25 = 225
4(c) Using the algebraic identity (𝑎 − 𝑏)2 = 𝑎2 − 2𝑎𝑏 + 𝑏2, evaluate 162
Since 16 can be rewritten as 20 − 4 and the question demanding us to evaluate 162, it
now resembles the algebraic identity (𝑎 − 𝑏)2 and therefore can be evaluated as
follows:
(20 − 4)2 = 202 − 2(4)(20) + 42 = 400 − 160 + 16 = 256

Factorisation (in this case) is the act of extracting common factors from algebraic
expression to be expressed with multiple brackets multiplied together. It is the absolute
reverse of expansion.
Example 5 [Simple Extraction of Common Factors]
Factorise 2𝑎2 − 8𝑎 completely.
The above question can be solved by simple extraction of 2𝑎 as common factor as noted
below:
2𝑎2 − 8𝑎 = 2𝑎(𝑎 − 4)
Example 6 [Factoring by Algebraic Identities]
(a) Factorise 49 − 4𝑥2 completely
(b) Factorise 9𝑥2 + 30𝑥 + 25 completely
(c) Factorise 9𝑥2 − 30𝑥 + 25 completely
Example 6(a) can be solved by applying the identity 𝑎2 − 𝑏2 = (𝑎 + 𝑏)(𝑎 − 𝑏) as tested
below:
𝑎 = 7
𝑏 = 2𝑥
𝑎2 − 𝑏2 = (7 − 2𝑥)(7 + 2𝑥)
49 − 4𝑥2 = (7 − 2𝑥)(7 + 2𝑥)
Example 6(b) can be solved by applying the identity 𝑎2 + 2𝑎𝑏 + 𝑏2 = (𝑎 + 𝑏)2 as
tested below:
𝑎 = √9𝑥2 = 3𝑥
𝑏 = √25 = 5
2𝑎𝑏 = 2(3𝑥)(5) = 30𝑥
∴ 9𝑥2 + 30𝑥 + 25 = (3𝑥 + 5)2
Example 6(c) can be solved by applying the identity of 𝑎2 − 2𝑎𝑏 + 𝑏2 = (𝑎 − 𝑏)2 as
tested below:
𝑎 = √9𝑥2 = 3𝑥
𝑏 = √25 = 5
−2𝑎𝑏 = 2(3𝑥)(5) = −30𝑥
∴ 9𝑥2 − 30𝑥 + 25 = (3𝑥 − 5)2

Example 7 (Factoring by Grouping Method)
(a) 9𝑥2 − 36𝑥𝑦 + 4𝑦 − 𝑥
(b) 12𝑝𝑟𝑠 + 4𝑞 + 3𝑞𝑟𝑠 + 16𝑝
7(a)
9𝑥2 − 36𝑥𝑦 + 4𝑦 − 𝑥
= 9𝑥2 − 𝑥 − 36𝑥𝑦 + 4𝑦 [Rearrange the expression]
= 𝑥(9𝑥 − 1) − 4𝑦(9𝑥 − 1)
= (𝑥 − 4𝑦)(9𝑥 − 1)
7(b)
12𝑝𝑟𝑠 + 4𝑞 + 3𝑞𝑟𝑠 + 16𝑝
= 12𝑝𝑟𝑠 + 3𝑞𝑟𝑠 + 4𝑞 + 16𝑝 [Rearrange the expression]
= 3𝑟𝑠(4𝑝 + 𝑞) + 4(𝑞 + 4𝑝)
= 3𝑟𝑠(4𝑝 + 𝑞) + 4(4𝑝 + 𝑞)
= (3𝑟𝑠 + 4)(4𝑝 + 𝑞)
